# Private background / discoverable material experiment

Status: **0.14.34 release gate passed** after the recovery work recorded below:
817 deterministic tests and 17/17 accepted fixed live planner reviews. Earlier
failed gates remain in this record. No live chat or saved setting was modified.
See the [final results and scope of the release decision](#release-gate-result).

## Scope

Inspired by the separation of private world progression from observable evidence
in [Story Engine](https://github.com/ZDOSt/Story-Engine/blob/cf6980786db9f464e71c88c1bcc6728152f57e9b/world-memory.js),
not its immutable scene outcomes, mechanics, prose guard or writing-style layer.
No dependency was installed or source copied into TF.

The existing single planner response now maintains enduring subjects, a private
background snapshot and a selected integrated horizon packet. Background records
carry a provisional independent development, a causal/time basis and a current
access route. A `none` route keeps the subject private without retiring it.
Selected subject ids must refer only to accessible subjects. Private owners,
progression and discovery rationale are not serialized into the writer packet.
Background updates are archived separately and never create witnessed progress.

No global world clock, turn-count advancement, forced discovery, additional
model call, repair loop, auto-retry or new setting is added. The semantic truth
of a time/access assessment remains model-dependent; validation proves structural
consistency, not that a claimed opportunity is really plausible.

## Predeclared live checks

- Five-stage garden/ferry fixture: inaccessible independent repairs while a local
  conversation continues; no acceleration within one cup of tea; elapsed days
  without discovery; a later actual notice opens access; staying local preserves
  optional wider possibilities without booking a journey or declaring repairs
  complete. Fixed synthetic accepted play, not writer-generated events.
- Saved-RP review from its existing preparation, plus nonhuman wetland and closed
  one-evening premises. Planner only; no writer call or browser integration claim.
- Each review gets one configured provider request. Any failed output stays in
  its report and is not repaired or silently replaced.

## Initial failure and bounded revision

The first garden/ferry trial used bundle
`e19f2538faedb6053060196a6e8d8cf4e75cc3cc75da63b4fdf9b5ad12248bb8`.
It marked ferry access `none` but included the ferry subject and its future reach
in selected horizons. Strict validation rejected the whole response, retaining
the old state. The failure demonstrates why a prompt-only privacy claim is not
enough. Artifacts: `%TEMP%/tf-discovery-background-20260919`.

The first revision orders developments and background before selected material
in the response schema and explicitly excludes inaccessible contributors from
all three selected horizons. Evaluation uses a new artifact directory; the failed
run remains intact. There is no automatic second attempt in runtime.

That revision (bundle
`f0c95bfe78e6ccba68dacaf9ff4bc968854017cfd5e9797331d42d87c0f52748`)
avoided selecting the hidden ferry, but treated the separate background list as
offscreen-only: it created a garden aim and an unmatched ferry background record.
Validation rejected the response. Artifacts:
`%TEMP%/tf-discovery-background-r2-20260919`.

The representation was then simplified instead of adding another retry or
weakening validation: each subject contains its own background/access record.
The model returns a full private subject snapshot before selecting material.
Omitted retained aims are rejected unless explicitly retired with exact evidence.
The storage adapter persists only changed aims and archives background separately;
an unchanged full snapshot does not multiply private aim archives. This removes
the second parallel list of ids that the model had to maintain consistently.

## Fresh-selection revision

Rather than feeding the old injection and local scene back as a template, input
now retains durable aims/background and witnesses but withholds both transient
drafts. Current accepted messages follow static source and prior preparation.
The contract distinguishes access to a trace/opportunity from access to its hidden
cause or completion, retains important independent source projects before discovery,
and respects closed scope in the schema as well as the system prompt. No repair,
extra provider request or semantic filter was added.

The same predeclared fixtures run in fresh directories. A fixture-launch mistake
attempted to append the initial empty stage and was rejected before any provider
request; the frozen directory was then used for its ordinary initial review.
The model trials remain one request per review.

Bundle `67f8e6cd3b1b68ace8f79c1e5217bc3d64e29b9fbf49106a451cbc25f0dbc7ea`
produced six accepted responses out of eight requests, not eight passes. Artifacts:
`%TEMP%/tf-discovery-fresh[-closed|-ecosystem|-real]-20260919`.

- The five-stage progression retained the ferry from the first pass, kept it out
  of early selections, used the posted opportunity after discovery and updated
  to actual dye testing rather than stale seedling talk. No journey was booked.
  Before discovery, its access label still incorrectly said `information` despite
  admitting there was no relevant notice; this did not leak into selection.
- The two-days-later response added an undeclared `facts and limits only` property
  inside episode and was rejected. The previous state remained intact; the next
  scheduled fixture review later succeeded without repairing this response.
- The closed evening correctly returned no subjects or injection. The ecological
  packet contained natural processes and no invented human participant.
- The saved RP response used finite episode names as realization subject ids.
  The storage adapter then supplied missing subject placeholders, exceeding its
  array bound. It was rejected without changing saved state. Its draft injection
  had wider reach, but an unaccepted draft is not a successful result.

A final bounded revision removes the wording copied into the spurious property,
clarifies the realization subject/episode distinction in the schema, and validates
those ids before adaptation so errors identify the real problem. It also makes
`information` require an existing relevant surface, not just a future news channel.
Validation remains strict; no response is repaired or retried.

## Recovery: private updates and atomic closure

The failed gate is preserved above, not replaced by a later successful sample.
The next revision changes the transaction, not just the prompt:

- Private aims use explicit upsert semantics. Omission keeps an aim dormant;
  the current selection still must be authored in full on every review. Every
  selected contributor requires freshly assessed background in that response.
- Retirement overrides an overlapping private update. The adapter no longer
  invents active-selection placeholders for retired subjects. Same-pass final
  progress validates against the retiring subject before its evidence is kept
  privately; it cannot leave selected material behind. Invalid final witnesses
  still reject the complete transaction.
- An unreviewed legacy aim remains private with no assessed access. No model
  repair, invented fictional progress or automatic second request is involved.
- The selected available field asks for opportunities and their conditions,
  not a recap of accomplishments. The contract distinguishes authority or
  competence to do work from proof that it is completed, and explicitly gives
  player interests world/NPC support rather than assigning them NPC ownership.

Deterministic regressions cover omission, overlapping retirement with and without
final evidence, rollback, legacy migration, actual host commit and metadata/cache
reload. The unchanged 17-stage live gate is rerun in fresh external directories
`%TEMP%/tf-discovery-recovery-{case}-20260919`; earlier failures remain available.

### Recovery results and final transaction changes

The recovery bundle `74058f1eaba3f0923af137f9317ebb08e8c0e0d5196d1b6cfb9fa64b68484bc0`
accepted **15/17** requests. Retirement/omission no longer blocked the four earlier
transition points, and the workshop injection no longer claimed the delegated
invoice correction was completed. Two different failures remained:

- Touring's later participation observation used `participating` after `partial`.
  The ordinal ledger treated this as lost progress and rejected otherwise current
  guidance. Continuing participation is compatible with partial accomplishment:
  the ledger now joins those observations, retaining the achieved partial status,
  its original witness and bounded recent witnesses. Reintroduction and reopening
  closed episodes still fail. No accomplishment is inferred from participation.
- The saved-RP response retyped formatted table fragments that were not contiguous
  source quotes. Exact-witness validation correctly rejected them. The final wire
  uses numbered source spans instead: the model cites a supplied message index and
  span number, and the application resolves the exact original text. Missing ids
  fail atomically; there is no fuzzy search, invented quote, memory substitution or
  model repair. The existing saved ledger and exact-witness validation stay intact.

The new prompt representation preserves source text and markup in bounded exact
spans, budgets that representation, and retains speaker attribution. A citation
still does **not** prove that the model interpreted its meaning correctly. The
contract explicitly distinguishes enacted substance from pending work.

Semantic inspection also found an unsupported second garden in the housemate
packet, and garden interests suppressed by an inaccessible ferry project in early
background reviews. The final contract preserves ambiguous assets as ambiguous
and considers available source interests as well as inaccessible future projects.

The fixed 17-request gate is repeated once more under the release version, without
discarding these failures, in `%TEMP%/tf-horizons-release-{case}-20260919`. It uses
the same accepted trajectories, source settings, single-request policy and no RP
writer calls. Final results follow below.

### Remaining model limitations

- The everyday sample still inferred a separate flat garden from an ambiguous
  reference to "the garden", despite the ambiguity constraint. It did not assign
  a player decision or schedule a scene, but asset identity was not reliably
  grounded. This is a known semantic limitation, not something exact citations
  or schema validation prove correct.
- Some packets are wordier than ideal and include optional action examples or
  broad paired possibilities. They do not supply ordered beats or exhaustive
  success/failure scripts, but the contract cannot guarantee perfect prose from
  every model response.
- Witnesses establish exact source attribution, not infallible interpretation.
  Private time/access assessments also remain provisional model judgments.
- No RP writer was invoked and no real browser World Info/Continuity assembly
  was reproduced. The saved-RP check tests its frozen source and existing plan;
  it does not prove how the next live reply will use the guidance.

### Decision

Release 0.14.34 for the requested guidance/maintenance changes. The transition
blockers are repaired, the one-call workflow holds across the fixed trajectory,
and inspected packets retain substantive mid-/long-term room without forcing
departures, encounters, outcomes or writing-style instructions. The remaining
model-grounding imperfection is disclosed rather than relabeled a passed factual
check. Earlier failed gates are not replaced or hidden by this decision.

Deployment is a commit from the main source repository, fast-forward alignment
of published `main` and `testing`, then a fast-forward Git pull in ST's extension
checkout. Reload ST to load the browser code; normal automatic review adopts the
new representation without rewriting the saved RP or planner settings. The server
plugin has only a version-label change and no new backend requirement.
