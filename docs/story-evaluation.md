# Story quality per token

Offline tests protect deterministic mechanics. They cannot prove that a writing model produces better stories. Use a short paired evaluation before publishing: keep the character card, transcript, player turns, provider/model, settings, and sampling parameters identical between the previous build and this one. Use separate chat copies; do not overwrite a live story. Repeat each case at least three times and hide build labels while scoring.

| Case | Fixed situation | Desired result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quiet landing | Two people finish tea; the user explicitly asks to stay peacefully in the room. | A natural completion, useful detail, or change in NPC stance; no forced visitor, danger, interrogation, or player emotion. |
| Repetitive motion | Three replies repeat a glance, smile, and cup adjustment without changing anything. | The audit identifies the repeated pattern; the next reply gains substance through the existing activity rather than surprise drama. |
| Sleeping through a quiet interval | In a school music-club slice-of-life scene, the player explicitly sleeps until morning; ordinary preparations for the next rehearsal are already established. Compare fresh-context and rules-only replies. | The authorized interval produces a fitting lasting difference, not repeated breathing or bedding descriptions. No forced danger, interruption, invented dreams/feelings/choices, or knowledge of unseen events; any development becomes knowable through the established viewpoint or a later learning route. |
| Separate relatives | Mira's father is a doctor; Lena's father is a sailor. The user suggests visiting Lena's father after Mira previously suggested a walk. | Both fathers and both proposal sources remain distinct. A variant explicitly makes Mira and Bea siblings; shared parentage is preserved too. |
| Private suspicion | Mira privately compares two ledgers and suspects fraud. Ari has not seen them. | Suspicion shapes Mira's behavior without becoming proven fraud or knowledge granted to Ari. An on-screen explanation later supplies a learning route. |
| Deferred town | A remote harbor had delayed shipments; the player remains at tea, then later skips sixteen days and asks about it. | Silence while irrelevant; a bounded, uncertain update when relevant, with old settlements retained and no scheduled catastrophe. |
| Full board | Twelve unresolved offscreen subjects exist; a new relevant subject appears. | The new subject enters active attention; displaced history remains recoverable without injecting the entire archive. |
| Observing a chase | A nonfighter player watches a fleeing thief and guards already pursuing. Use “I observe”, then “I keep watching”. | Connected visible pursuit developments without requiring commands, making the player follow/fight, repeating the setup, or revealing unseen escape details. The chase can leave view or resolve without player involvement. |
| NPC-only conversation | Two colleagues discuss a delivery while the player listens. | The colleagues can decide, disagree, finish, and return to work without requiring the player to mediate. Their personalities and duties affect willingness. |
| NPC disengagement | A friendly but busy merchant has answered questions and owes a delivery. The player watches rather than issuing another command. | The merchant can politely end the exchange and leave; later replies/reloads retain the absence and commitment. No automatic reappearance or permanent availability. |
| Player disengagement | The player declines an optional invitation and explicitly leaves. | No repeated replacement hooks, forced consent, or invented punishment/pursuit. Existing NPC goals continue without assigning player thoughts or a new destination. |
| Constrained departure | Established guards are blocking an exit; the player attempts to leave. | Their established opposition matters without automatic escape or authoring the player's compliance, feelings, or contested response. Do not invent a new obstacle solely to prevent departure. |
| Timely intervention | During a chase, the player explicitly interrupts a guard or attempts an intervention. | Incorporate the intervention before advancing beyond its opportunity; do not race through the whole chase or decide the player's uncertain result. |
| Quiet observer | The player watches a familiar tea ritual, or waits for an explicitly stated short interval. | Task-native progress or a natural landing, not manufactured danger, endless gestures, omniscience, or an inferred long time skip. |
| Changed willingness | A normally reserved NPC has a supported reason to speak; a friendly NPC has a supported reason to refuse. | Contextual personality rather than rigid archetypes; neither universal compliance nor mandatory disengagement. |

Score each continuation 0–2 on continuity, player agency/knowledge boundaries, scene fit, meaningful development, and repetition avoidance. A quiet scene can score fully without escalation. Any agency violation, false knowledge transfer, or lost explicit correction is a failure regardless of total score.

For the GM cases, also score autonomous world initiative, reciprocal disengagement, and preserved intervention opportunities (0–2 each). Repeat observation and refusal across several replies; a single good continuation does not establish that the world avoids stalling or that departures persist. Compare missing/failed planner runs too: permanent GM rules should remain, but stale world facts must not be injected. These are model-quality checks, not claims that offline prompt assertions prove live behavior.

Record planner input/output tokens, number of requests (including retries), planner elapsed time, injected token count, and median quality scores. Check a sequence longer than twelve accepted replies so a broad review is included. Compare total cost per accepted reply, not just a single successful request or the configured ceilings. Reduced cost is useful only if continuity and scene quality do not regress.

Also exercise an interrupted planner, an edited user correction, and a swipe. Stale results must not overwrite newer facts; retries must not make generation wait; a replacement should reuse its archived causal slice rather than recanonize discarded prose. These behaviors have offline coverage but still need a SillyTavern integration smoke test before release.
